How one company adopts DataOS

An example journey for sales · 18 November 2026

This document follows one company from the first engineer who tries DataOS to a company-wide contract. At each step it shows what the company does, what we see in the product, and what our team does.

It applies the plan in "DataOS for Engineering Leaders", section 5. The company, the people and the numbers are made up. The pattern is the one we expect in mid-size enterprises, the segment where we land first.

The journey in four lines
  • Try: one engineer deploys an agent on DataOS for free, on sample data.
  • Share: the engineer connects real company systems and shares the agent. This step crosses the wall, the line between a personal test and real company use. It is also the first payment.
  • Spread: more teams and non-engineers build. Our sellers see it in product use and start to work the account.
  • Standardize: the engineering leader signs a company plan. Revenue then grows with the number of builders.

Today exists in DataOS nowNew work part of the plan, not yet built

Terms used below

AgentAn AI program that does a task for a person, for example a draft quote from company data.
BuilderA person who creates or changes an agent on DataOS in a given month. Customers pay for builders.
ViewerA person who only uses agents. Viewers are free.
Free tierOne engineer builds and runs agents for themselves, on sample data.
Team planThe first payment. Agents connect to real company systems and can be shared. Paid by card, per builder, without a sales call.
Company planA company-wide contract signed by the engineering leader. It adds the controls that a security team asks for.
Audit logThe record of who used which agent, and what data the agent read or changed for them.
Data productCompany data packaged with its definitions, owner and quality checks, so that people and agents use the same numbers.
Agent inventoryOne list of every agent in the company, with who built it, who uses it and what data it reaches.

2The company

Norrvik Industries (made up) makes industrial pumps and valves. It has 4,000 staff, nine plants in North America, Europe and Asia, and sells through a direct sales team and distributors. It already pays for Microsoft 365 Copilot. Staff built many Copilot agents and small AI-built apps, and nobody has a full list of them.

The platform team has six engineers. They run the cloud, the integrations and the data warehouse. Each week they get requests to "host this thing someone built". Most of those requests wait for weeks in security review.

The data that agents need sits in the usual places for a manufacturer:

  • Orders, prices, inventory and production plans in SAP.
  • Accounts, opportunities and quotes in Salesforce.
  • Product specifications and past quotes in SharePoint.
  • Staff and skills in the HR system, and finance in Snowflake.

Some of this data is restricted. A regional sales team must see only its own region's prices and margins. Distributor discounts are confidential to each distributor. HR data is visible only to managers for their own staff.

3The steps

Each step shows what happens, which plan the company is on, and what our team does. The tags under each step show which capabilities exist today and which are new work.

TryOne engineer, no payment, no approval
1

An agent that works for one person

Priya builds a quote agent on her laptop with an agent framework. A customer sends a request for quote. The agent then finds similar past quotes in SharePoint, reads list prices and lead times from SAP, and drafts a quote. It works only because it runs with Priya's own logins. DataOS is not involved yet.

2

Colleagues want the agent

Priya shows the agent at a sales meeting. Tom wants his team to use it for a large distributor tender. Her options are poor:

  • Share her logins. Everyone then sees every region's prices and every distributor's discount.
  • Ask for a server. That means a ticket, a security review and weeks of waiting.
  • Rebuild it in Copilot Studio. That means starting again in a different tool.

She searches for a way to host an internal agent behind the company login, and finds DataOS.

3

Deploy on the free tier, on sample data

Priya signs up alone and deploys her agent's code to DataOS. The free tier does not connect to real company systems. She tests the agent on sample sales data from a DataOS template. The agent runs, and the audit log records each action. DataOS also offers templates, which are working agents that anyone can start from and change. One of them is a quote agent. Priya does not need one because she has her own code.

PlanFree tier. No payment, nobody approves anything.
Our teamNo person acts. The product, the templates and the docs do the work.
New work Agent hosting, free tier, templates Today Agents read data products through MCP
ShareThe wall: real systems and other users
4

Connect real systems and share (first payment)

To use real data and to share the agent, Priya needs the team plan. She opens one for the platform team and pays by card. It is the only way to meet Tom's deadline. She connects SharePoint, SAP and Salesforce.

Each person on Tom's team signs in with their work Microsoft account. The agent then works as that person:

  • It reaches each system with that person's own permissions. A sales rep who cannot see European margins in SAP cannot get them through the agent.
  • The audit log records every question and every record that the agent reads, against the person who asked.

5

The same team asks for more

Tom's team asks for a second agent. It prepares a brief before a customer visit, from the order history in SAP, open opportunities in Salesforce and recent service cases. Priya builds it on the same plan, with the same sign-in, the same per-person access and the same audit log.

Our teamLeo watches use. Viewers return each week, which shows that the agents create value.
SpreadMore teams, more builders, the first sales conversation
6

A second team asks for its own

Procurement hears about the quote agent. It wants an agent that flags supplier risk from purchase orders, delivery dates and supplier contracts. Contract terms are confidential, so each buyer must see only the suppliers that they manage.

Marcus builds it on DataOS, and the team plan now has two builders. Sign-in, per-person access and the audit log come with every agent on DataOS. The CISO's team now reviews only what each agent does, because DataOS already secures how every agent runs. The platform team writes a rule: "If you share an agent that uses company data, ship it on DataOS." Reviews that took weeks now take days.

PlanTeam plan. Two builders.
Our teamThis is the strongest signal: a second team, and the security team signed in to review agents. Leo asks for a meeting with the platform lead to learn what they plan next.
7

People outside engineering start to build

Ana, an operations manager at a plant, wants an agent that explains unplanned downtime from production and maintenance data. She starts from a DataOS template and changes it with an AI coding assistant. She does not file a ticket.

When she shares the agent, she becomes the third builder. Her agent gets the same sign-in, per-person access and audit log as Priya's. The platform team sees it in its list of agents without having built it. Other teams follow the same route. After some months, about a dozen agents run on DataOS.

PlanTeam plan. Three or more builders, about a dozen agents.
Our teamLeo and the platform lead agree on a usage report: which agents run, who uses them and what data they reach. The platform lead will take it to the CIO.
8

The sprawl reaches the CIO

Sprawl is what happens after a company gives everyone tools to build agents. The company then has many agents in many tools, and nobody can list them, trust them, maintain them or say what they cost. Three events land on Rosa's desk:

  • The platform team gets a page at 2 a.m. An app that 200 people use breaks. A planner who left the company built it in another tool, and the platform team never approved it.
  • A large automotive customer sends a security questionnaire. It asks Norrvik to list every AI tool that touches the customer's data, and to show who accessed what. Rosa cannot answer for the Copilot agents.
  • The bills arrive. The company pays for Copilot and for AI tools on team credit cards. Nobody can say which agents people use, or which ones are worth the cost.

The one part of the company's AI that Rosa can account for is the dozen agents on DataOS. The usage report from step 7 shows who built each agent, who uses it and what data it reached.

StandardizeThe engineering leader makes DataOS the standard
9

The CIO signs the company plan

Rosa and the CISO review DataOS once, as a platform, instead of reviewing agents one at a time. Rosa signs the company plan. It adds what the team plan does not have:

  • Company login through Norrvik's identity system, and automatic staff sync. Joiners get access and leavers lose it without manual work.
  • The agent inventory: one list of every agent in the company. It covers agents on DataOS. It is also meant to list the Copilot agents. How DataOS reads them is an open question.
  • Approval before go-live for any agent that touches customer or price data.
  • Company-wide policies set once in DataOS, for example regional price rules applied to every agent.
  • Audit export. Norrvik answers the customer questionnaire from the audit log.

The rule from step 6 grows: "Any agent shared beyond its builder runs on DataOS or appears in the agent inventory." The app from the 2 a.m. page moves to DataOS.

PlanCompany plan, signed by the CIO.
Our teamLeo runs the deal with the CIO, the CISO and procurement. Our engineers support the security review.
New work Staff sync, agent inventory, approvals, central policies, audit export
10

One set of numbers for every agent

The inventory shows a new problem. Three agents report three different margin figures, because each one calculates margin its own way. The data team publishes margin, on-time delivery and inventory as data products on DataOS, each with one definition and one owner. Agents read the data products through MCP, the open standard that connects agents to data. Every agent and every report then uses the same numbers.

This step uses the part of DataOS that customers run in production today. It is also where DataOS grows from agents into business meaning, as the plan expects.

PlanCompany plan. The data team joins as builders.
Our teamOur engineers help the data team publish the first data products. This opens the larger data platform conversation.
Today Data products, agent access through MCP
11

Open DataOS to the whole company

With the company plan in place, Rosa opens DataOS to everyone at once instead of team by team. Each function gets templates and a build week. The platform team now curates templates and approves agents that touch customer or price data. It no longer hosts or reviews each agent by hand.

PlanCompany plan. Builders grow each month. Viewers stay free.
Our teamCustomer success tracks builders and active agents, and plans the renewal from that use.
12

Norrvik becomes a reference

5Where it ends

Every agent that Norrvik shares runs on DataOS or appears in the agent inventory. Copilot agents stay in Copilot. DataOS lists them but does not run them. The platform team's fears each have an answer:

Fear of the platform teamWhat changed
Blamed for something they never approvedEvery agent on DataOS passed one platform review. Agents that touch customer or price data are approved before go-live.
Paged for apps they did not buildShared agents run on one platform with monitoring. The inventory flags agents whose builder left the company.
Agents see more data than they needEvery agent on DataOS works with the permissions of the person who uses it.
Seen as the team that only blocksSharing an agent takes days, not weeks in review.
No view of the costThe inventory shows which agents people use. Builders are the only paid unit.

Appendix: open questions

The plan does not need these answers now. We will answer them as we build and learn from the first customers.

  • Prices for the team plan per builder, and for the company plan.
  • Whether a price per builder pushes a company to keep builders few, and whether that slows step 11.
  • How often a company blocks card payments for software, and so stops step 4.
  • How the agent inventory reads agents built in Copilot Studio and other tools. The paper sets the goal of one place to govern agents built anywhere, but not the method.
  • Who owns templates when there are many: the platform team, each function, or both.
