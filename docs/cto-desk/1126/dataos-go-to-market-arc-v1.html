How DataOS reaches its buyers

Discovery, launch and campaigns · 18 November 2026

This document answers two questions. How do we find the engineering leaders who need DataOS? And how do they find us? It proposes one arc, from a quiet start to a category that DataOS owns.

It builds on "DataOS for Engineering Leaders", section 5, and on How one company adopts DataOS. Like the paper, it is a plan to align on. It sets no dates and no budgets.

The arc in five lines
  • Get ready: give DataOS its own site, docs, templates and a way to score every sign-up.
  • Design partners: we find ten to twenty companies ourselves and put real agents in production with them.
  • Launch: one launch week, one big idea about agent sprawl, and a report and a free tool to carry it.
  • Always on: engineers find us through search, templates, registries, peers and vendors. Sellers act on product signals.
  • Own the category: public customers, analysts and a yearly report make DataOS the name for this space.

Terms used below

The podThe small team that runs DataOS end to end: product, engineering, marketing and sales.
Target profileThe companies that we sell to first: mid-size, non-tech enterprises with several data platforms and a visible AI program.
Design partnerAn early customer that builds real agents with our engineers and tells us what to change.
Free tierOne engineer builds and runs agents on DataOS for free, only for themselves.
The wallThe point where an agent reaches private company systems and other people use it. It is where a company starts to pay.
Self-serveA company signs up, tries DataOS and pays without a sales call.
Product-led salesSellers choose which company to talk to, and when, from what people do in the product.
MCPThe open standard that connects AI agents to tools and data.
GateA checkpoint at the end of a phase. The next phase starts only when its goal is met.

1Two questions, one pod

Discovery works in two directions. In the first direction, we find buyers. The pod looks for companies that fit the target profile and shows them something useful. In the second direction, buyers find us. An engineer searches for a way to solve a problem today, and DataOS is the answer that they find.

The target profile comes from the paper. In those companies, the engineering leader is the buyer. The engineer on the platform team is the first user.

Early on, most customers come from our own search. Later, most come from the product and from search engines. The arc below moves the weight from the first direction to the second. The pod records where each paying company came from, so the numbers show when the weight moves.

The problem is the first row. Today, an engineering leader who searches for DataOS lands on a business story. The paper says that each persona hears one story, from the brand to the first login. So the brand map comes before every other step in this plan.

4The five phases

Each phase lists what we do, how we find buyers, how buyers find us, and the gate that ends it.

Phase 1: Get readyQuiet. No public announcement
1

Build the front door and the discovery machine

  • Give DataOS its own domain and site, grown from the landing page prototype. Move the docs under it.
  • Change Modern's site so that its headline tells the business story and its technical links go to the DataOS site.
  • Publish a public repository of working agent templates, and one reference architecture for an agent in production behind company login.
  • Write the security pack, our set of documents for a security review.
  • Match every sign-up to its company through the work email. Add company size, industry and cloud from a data provider, and score the account against the target profile.
GateThe site, the docs, the templates and the security pack are ready, and the scoring works.
Who actsThe DataOS pod, with Modern marketing for the change to Modern's site.
Phase 2: Design partnersWe find buyers ourselves
2

Ten to twenty companies with real agents

The paper names three sources for the first design partners. They are the 40 current prospects of the sales team, introductions from Gensler and Lenovo, and engineering leaders that Modern knows. This plan adds signals that show the sprawl problem from the journey document:

  • Job posts for AI platform engineers.
  • New CIO and CTO hires in the target profile.
  • Public rollouts of Copilot or ChatGPT Enterprise.
  • Companies whose people take the Modern Data 101 maturity assessment or the AI readiness scorecard on Modern's site.

The offer is a working session on one agent that a team needs, not a sales pitch. With each partner's permission, we write field notes from the work. Those notes become the launch content. The DataOS site shows a waiting list, so that early interest has somewhere to go.

GateDesign partners run agents in production, and several agree to be named at launch.
Who actsThe pod's technical sellers and engineers.
Phase 3: LaunchThe one public moment
3

One launch week, one big idea

A launch week puts one new capability in front of engineers each day for five days. Supabase uses this pattern, and each day gives engineers a new reason to come back. The DataOS week follows the journey:

  1. Hosting for agents behind company login.
  2. Access with each person's own permissions.
  3. The audit log.
  4. Templates that anyone can start from.
  5. Data products through MCP, the part of DataOS that exists today.

The big idea is one question: "How many agents does your company run?" In most companies, nobody knows. Two assets carry the question:

  • A report on agent sprawl, from a survey of engineering leaders. Modern already publishes a yearly report, so the report reuses that work.
  • A free tool that lists the agents in a company, for example the Copilot Studio agents in a Microsoft 365 tenant. The tool is also a candidate entry product, because its result leads straight to the agent inventory. It needs a feasibility study first.

The week runs where engineers look: Show HN (the section of Hacker News for new products), Product Hunt, the MCP registries, a cloud marketplace and the open template repository. The design partners from phase 2 speak for us.

GateSign-ups from companies in the target profile reach the free tier, and some cross the wall.
Who actsThe whole pod, with Modern marketing for reach.
Phase 4: Always onBuyers find us
4

An engine that runs every week

After launch, five channels bring engineers to DataOS:

  • Search: technical writing on the exact problems that engineers search for. Examples are an internal agent behind single sign-on, authentication for MCP servers, and per-user permissions for agents.
  • Templates: a gallery of working agents, and listings in the integration directories of the tools that companies use.
  • Registries and marketplaces: MCP registries, agent tool directories and cloud marketplaces.
  • Peers: small roundtables for CIOs and CTOs in each target industry. The CIO in the journey document who speaks at a roundtable is this channel.
  • Vendors: AI vendors that build DataOS into their own agents, as described in the paper.

Our own discovery continues through product-led sales, which acts on the signals in the journey document. The pod records the source of each paying company and moves budget to the sources that work.

GateMost new paying companies come from self-serve and not from our own search.
Who actsThe pod's content, community and product-led sales roles.
Phase 5: Own the categoryDataOS becomes the name for this space
5

Teach the category, and repeat the report

  • Brief the analysts who track this space. Gartner already tracks AI application development platforms and AI governance platforms.
  • Publish case studies from the design partners, with their written approval.
  • Repeat the agent sprawl report each year, so that its numbers become the ones that people quote.
  • Use Modern Data 101 for the second half of the story. Agents come first, and data products follow. The data leaders in that community are the right audience for this step.
GateThe arc becomes the normal rhythm of the pod: a launch moment each year and the engine every week.
Who actsThe pod, with Modern for analysts and Modern Data 101.

7How we know that it works

The pod records the source of every sign-up and every paying company. Each phase has one main measure:

PhaseMain measure
Get readyEach item on the gate list is done
Design partnersDesign partners with agents in production
LaunchSign-ups from companies in the target profile
Always onShare of paying companies that come from self-serve
Own the categorySearches for DataOS, analyst mentions and inbound requests from target companies

The gates in the paper use the same rule. If most paying companies still come from our own search after the launch, the self-serve loop does not work yet, and the gate catches it.

8What we do not do

  • We do not launch DataOS to engineers through Modern's site. It mixes the two personas.
  • We do not use Modern Data 101 as the funnel for engineers. Its members are data people, and its value depends on its independence.
  • We do not launch before design partners run real agents. A launch without proof spends the one public moment.
  • We do not cold-call engineers. Sellers act on signals in the product, and they offer help, not a pitch.

Appendix: open questions

The plan does not need these answers now. We will answer them as the pod starts.

  • The domain and the name of the DataOS site.
  • How far Modern's site moves away from the DataOS name, and who decides.
  • Whether the free tool that lists agents can read Copilot Studio and other tools, and with which permissions.
  • How Modern Data 101 covers DataOS and still stays independent.
  • The marketing budget for a second brand, which the paper names as the cost of a separate brand.
  • The product readiness that each phase needs, so that the launch matches what DataOS can do.
