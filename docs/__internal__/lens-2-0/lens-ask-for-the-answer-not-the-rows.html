Ask for the answer, not the rows

Semantic models, the 50,000-row limit, and Tablet NPS across Quick and Power BI

By the end you'll know why a year-over-year widget in Quick hit a 50,000-row limit, which of two connections fits which job, and why one shared definition of Tablet NPS matters more when a company runs two BI tools.

A note on bias: the author co-founded Modern, which builds DataOS and Lens. This page explains a Modern product to a Modern customer. The pattern it argues for is widely used across the industry, and section 4 names other products that use it.
Who this is for: Lenovo's Quick authors, who can start with section 1 and section 5, and the BI and data owners who decide how metrics get defined, who will get most from section 3 and section 4. It assumes you know Quick or Power BI, not semantic models. It isn't a setup guide.

1 · What the limit is telling you

the cap is a design choice

Lenovo's Quick authors build dashboards on datasets sourced from data products. One widget compares Tablet NPS year over year. To build it, the dataset pulls individual survey responses through Lens, and Quick then works out the comparison itself, with a function like periodOverPeriodDifference. Two years of responses is far more than 50,000 rows, so the query stops.

Lens is built to return answers, and answers are small. NPS by region by month for two years is a few hundred rows. The cap exists so that Lens doesn't get used as a bulk export, because that job belongs to a different connection.

2 · Two doors into the same data

one for answers, one for rows

Lens sits between your tools and the data. The Lens documentation describes it as a modeling layer over warehouses and lakehouses, queried through Postgres-compatible SQL, REST and GraphQL. You ask in business terms (a measure, sliced by dimensions, filtered by a segment), and Lens writes the SQL for the engine underneath.

The 50,000-row limit is a signpost to the other door. The warehouse and its query engine hold the rows and hand them over as rows. Both doors are legitimate. The diagram shows where each one sits.

Quick, Power BI and agents all enter above the line, where definitions live. Data science and bulk extracts enter below it, including loads into SPICE (Quick's in-memory copy of a dataset, refreshed on a schedule). The year-over-year widget tried to use the answer door to do a row-door job.

3 · What a semantic model holds

tablet nps as the example

A semantic model is a written-down agreement about what a number means. For Tablet NPS it holds four kinds of thing. The measure is the formula: share of promoters minus share of detractors, worked out from response counts. The dimensions are the ways you slice it, such as region, product line and survey month. A segment is a saved filter, for example enterprise accounts only; the Lens documentation calls these predefined filters. Access rules decide who sees what, and a Lens model keeps them in the same folder as its tables and views.

NPS can't be averaged. Every author pulling raw rows has to remember that. Lens only has to remember it once. Averaging five regional scores gives the wrong global score, because the regions have different numbers of respondents. The right answer recalculates from the counts.

4 · One definition, every tool

why power bi makes this more urgent

Two tools, two definitions, and nothing to tell you when they disagree. Lenovo runs Power BI as well as Quick, and each tool has its own place to define metrics. Power BI keeps measures inside its semantic models, written in DAX. Quick keeps them as calculated fields inside each analysis. So Tablet NPS can be defined in both, by different people. The diagram compares that with one shared definition.

The left side is the usual state after a few years with two BI tools. The first sign of drift is two dashboards showing different NPS in the same meeting. On the right, the definition lives once, below both tools. Power BI connects to the same Lens model through BI Sync, and Quick reads the same measures through the SQL interface. When Lenovo adds AI agents, they ask the same model and get the same answer.

This is a widely accepted pattern today. LookML defines metrics once inside Looker. dbt's semantic layer does it in the transformation project. Databricks offers metric views in Unity Catalog, built in part for ratios and distinct counts that can't be safely re-added, the same problem as NPS. Apache Ossie, formerly the Open Semantic Interchange, is an open specification for writing these definitions down so they can move between tools. It joined the Apache Incubator in June 2026.

5 · Lens or warehouse

a rule for quick authors

Start with one test: would the result fit on a screen? If it would, it's a Lens question. The table gives the full rule. Each row is a yes-or-no check, and the two columns don't overlap.

Prefer Lens whenPrefer the warehouse when
The result is a number sliced by something (region, month, product) and fits on a screenYou need individual records, such as single survey responses or free-text comments
The metric appears in more than one dashboard, tool or teamYou're loading more than 50,000 rows into SPICE or another system
The metric can't be added up: NPS, ratios, averages, distinct countsThe job is data science or model training on row-level data
It compares periods: year over year, month over month, year to dateYou're exploring a question nobody has defined a metric for yet
Access rules must apply the same way in every toolYou're checking data quality or reconciling against a source system
An AI agent or an app will ask the questionYou need to join with data that isn't in the semantic model

If two people could compute it differently, it belongs in Lens. This tie-breaker settles most of the cases where authors are tempted to pull raw rows.

Explore on the warehouse. Promote to Lens the second time you need it. A question with no agreed metric starts on the warehouse. Once that metric shows up in a second dashboard, it moves into Lens, so the warehouse doesn't become a permanent way around the model.

Going direct doesn't mean going without rules. The warehouse's own access controls still apply, but they sit on tables, not on business definitions like NPS.

6 · What it costs, and what comes next

the honest trade

A semantic model takes work up front. Someone has to agree the NPS formula, name the dimensions and own the model. A measure nobody has modeled yet isn't available through Lens until someone does, so authors may wait on the data team for new metrics. Lens is also one more layer between tools and data that has to be kept running. In the author's experience, the time spent agreeing definitions once is less than the time spent later reconciling dashboards that disagree.

For Lenovo, the next steps are concrete. First, rebuild the Tablet NPS year-over-year widget on a Lens measure, in a working session with one or two Quick authors. Second, connect Power BI to the same model, so both tools show the same NPS. Third, write the table from section 5 into a one-page guide for authors. Separately, the 800 MB NPS file that hit the 200 MB upload limit should go into S3 and become a data product from there, since browser upload isn't the right path for files that size.
