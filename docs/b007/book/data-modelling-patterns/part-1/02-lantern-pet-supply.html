A modeling pattern is easy to admire in the abstract. Its strengths and costs only show on real data, with real keys that disagree, real values that change, and real rules that the data must obey. So every chapter in this book works on one business. This chapter introduces it.

Lantern Pet Supply is made up, but nothing about it is unusual. Its systems, its data quality problems, and its legal duties are the ones that any mid-size retailer in the United States has. Read this chapter once, and refer back to it when a later chapter mentions a person, a system, or an event.

2.1The business

Lantern sells food, toys, and supplies for pets. It has 85 stores in six states, with its head office in Oakland, California. It also runs a web shop. The web shop has an autoship option: a customer chooses a product and a frequency, such as every four weeks, and the order repeats on its own. Forty of the stores have a grooming salon, where groomers wash, trim, and weigh pets by appointment.

A SKU, or stock keeping unit, is one sellable product in one size, such as a 12 kg bag of a salmon kibble. Food is the biggest category by revenue. Food also carries a lot number, the production batch printed on the bag, which matters when a product is recalled.

2.3The people

A few people appear again and again. Their details are fixed, so that you can follow them from chapter to chapter.

Maria Lopez is a loyalty customer with the CRM ID C-000123. She has a golden retriever, Biscuit, and a cat, Pepper. She buys in stores and on the web shop, and she has three phone numbers. She moves twice in 2025, from Oakland to San Jose and then to Sacramento, where she starts to shop at a Happy Tails store as HT-88412.

Sam Ortiz is Maria's partner, CRM ID C-002981. In September 2025 he becomes a second owner of Biscuit, which tests every model's idea of the pet-to-owner relationship.

Dev Patel is a groomer at the Oakland store, employee ID E-1047. He is paid by the hour plus a commission on grooming services. He is also a loyalty customer, C-003377. That makes him the most interesting person in the book for privacy and payroll rules.

Priya Shah is a new web shop customer, C-004410. Her first order arrives before the CRM has her record, which tests how a model handles a fact that arrives before its context.

2.4The rules

Lantern's head office and most of its stores are in California. California has some of the strictest employment and privacy rules in the United States, and each rule becomes a requirement on the data.

Minimum wages differ by place and date. California sets a state minimum wage, and many cities, including Oakland, set a higher one. Each rate has an effective date, often 1 January. A pay rate is therefore a value that is valid for a period, not a single fact.

Overtime is daily as well as weekly. In California, overtime is due after 8 hours in one workday, as well as after 40 hours in a week, as the Labor Commissioner's overtime guidance explains. An employee must also get a meal break before the end of the fifth hour of work, per the meal period guidance. A late or missed break costs one extra hour of pay. So payroll needs every punch, with its exact time.

Payroll records must be kept. The federal FLSA record keeping rules require employers to keep payroll records for at least three years. A model must never lose them, even when other data is deleted.

Customers can ask for deletion. The California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA) gives customers the right to ask a business to delete their personal information, with exceptions such as data that the business must keep by law.

Card numbers never enter the warehouse. The PCI Data Security Standard makes stored card numbers expensive to protect. The tills send only the payment type and a token.

Recalls need a trace. When the FDA announces a pet food recall, it names a product and its lot numbers. Lantern must find every customer who bought an affected lot, in every channel.

WarningThe wage rates in this book are illustrative. Real rates change every year, and some change twice. Never use the examples as a source for payroll.

Summary

  • Lantern Pet Supply is a made-up US pet retailer with 85 stores, a web shop with autoship, and grooming salons. Every example in the book uses it.
  • Seven source systems feed the warehouse. Only the CRM and the web shop exist at the start, and each later system brings its own customer key.
  • Maria Lopez, Sam Ortiz, Dev Patel, and Priya Shah appear throughout the book, each with fixed details.
  • California wage, overtime, and meal break rules, payroll retention, the CCPA, PCI, and FDA recalls all become requirements on the data.
  • A fixed timeline of events, from Maria's moves to Dev's deletion request, drives the stress tests in chapter 5.
